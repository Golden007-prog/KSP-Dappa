# Phase 8 — analytical depth — decision entries

Voice and numbering follow docs/DECISIONS.md D-014..D-019; the lead renumbers. Every figure below was read from a live call to `catalyst serve --http 3105` (Development Data Store, 28 Aug 2026) or from `docs/benchmarks/*.json` written by `pipeline/depth_benchmarks.py` the same day.

**D-phase8-depth-1 · Careers are rebuilt from NetworkEdge, whole careers only, with an oversize-career guard (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
No stored table carries "who did what, when" per offender: OffenderProfile has no case ids and NetworkEdge lists case ids only per co-accused pair. `lib/depth/corpus.js` walks NetworkEdge (ordered by PersonKeyA, tieBreak PersonKeyB; default 9,000 rows = 30 ZCQL pages), collects each person's case ids from both ends, admits persons in RiskScore order until a 2,400-case budget is spent, and fetches CaseMaster in 80-id IN-chunks four at a time. A person enters with every edge-visible case or not at all, because a trajectory measured on half a history is not a trajectory. Careers with more than 60 edge-visible cases are skipped: on the live store they are identity-resolution over-merges (one "person" carrying 136–230 FIRs; see entry 4) and admitting them spent the whole budget on 14 names. What the live store returned: 9,000 of 23,833 edges (truncated, reported), 2,048 profiles, 1,436 persons with edges, 442 admitted, 14 skipped as oversize, 980 skipped for budget, 2,400 cases resolved; cold call 4–8 s, then cached 15 min per container. The corpus is a bounded sample and every answer says so in `scan`; the limits are query params (`maxEdges` up to 24,000, `maxCases` up to 4,000).

**D-phase8-depth-2 · One gravity ladder for the corpus matrix and the per-offender card (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
The client's BehaviourChangeCard already scores offence seriousness 1–10 by sub-head name (`routes/offenders/behaviour.js` GRAVITY). `lib/depth/common.js` pins the same scale by sub-head id, banded into four rungs (petty 1–3, moderate 4–5, serious 6–8, heinous 9–10), so the corpus transition matrix and Offender 360's staircase agree on every rung. Live: 3,129 case-to-case steps, 31.5 % up a rung, 31.2 % down, 37.3 % level; from petty the next case stays petty 53 % of the time and jumps to heinous 7 %.

**D-phase8-depth-3 · The escalation watchlist carries a coverage guard (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
Because the edge walk is bounded and only cases with a co-accused are edge-visible, a person can be measured on a thin slice of a long career: the first live watchlist led with a 53-case profile read on 6 dated cases. The watchlist now keeps only people whose dated cases cover at least half of their profile CaseCount or number ten or more, and the table shows "cases seen / profile cases" beside every name. Live after the guard: 149 careers with 3+ dated cases, 62 escalating, 48 winding down, 39 steady; 40 on the watchlist, led by a 64-case profile read on 20 dated cases (mean gravity 3.1 → 5.8, a rung climbed in July 2026).

**D-phase8-depth-4 · The identity sweep reproduces the pipeline's operating point exactly and shows the exact-name anchor pass is what costs precision (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
`pipeline/depth_benchmarks.py` re-implements `resolve_identities` (phonetic + gender blocking, 0.6·token_sort_ratio + 0.2·age + 0.2·district, exact-name anchor) and sweeps thresholds 0.70–0.96. At 0.92 the replica returns precision 0.0321 / recall 0.5712 / F1 0.0608 — the documented `identity_metrics.json` numbers to four places, so the sweep is the same scorer, not a look-alike. Three variants are published: v1 (deployed), v2 (scored pairs and anchors only within a district), v3 (anchor pass removed). The anchor pass merges 241,364 same-sorted-name pairs within two years of age across districts (27,355 inside one district); removing it lifts the best F1 from 0.074 (v1 at 0.94) to 0.354 (v3 at 0.94) with precision 0.30 and recall 0.44, and shrinks the largest cluster at 0.92 from 232 people to 70. The pipeline itself is not changed in this phase (Data Store rows would have to be regenerated and re-imported); the finding is on screen with the curve, and the per-alias card shows the mechanism on a live profile (P00204 "Rajesh Byadgi", 32 own rows, 265 co-accused names, "Ramesh Byadgi" linked at 0.934).

**D-phase8-depth-5 · Band coverage and drift are an offline replay because ForecastMonthly stores no backtest bands (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
`build_forecasts` keeps only the holdout point forecast for MAPE and writes no lo/hi for past months; live `/forecast` returns `mape: null` for every series. The generator refits the pipeline's own model choice on the 36 − 6 months and keeps the 80 % interval: coverage 0.697 over 1,368 held-out points (target 0.80; ETS(A,Ad,A,12) 0.533 on 85 series, seasonal-naive 0.795 on 143), median MAPE 47.78 (the documented figure, reproduced), MAPE falling from 58.2 % one month ahead to 49.6 % six ahead (no growth with horizon), 122 of 228 series under-covered. The API serves the JSON from the function bundle and adds a live cross-check from ForecastMonthly (228 series, 143 naive / 85 ETS, mean band width 166 % of the point forecast, 0 stored backtest rows); when a store does carry backtest rows (the fixture does) the same route computes stored coverage and MAPE by month.

**D-phase8-depth-6 · The recovery card reports floors and names its matching rules (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
Planted patterns come from `pipeline/dappa_seed.py` (HOTSPOTS, ANOMALIES, FINAL8_START) and `_ground_truth_offenders.csv`; the generator does not persist its `tag` column, so recovery is measured against the outputs. Hotspots: 1 of 6 has a DBSCAN cluster of the same crime head within 1.5 km (Devaraja Market, 0.05 km) — the cluster step ran on the last 6 months while the planted spots span 36 — but all 6 planted centres are the densest 1-km cell for their sub-head and hour band (149–165 cases within 1 km), so the signal is in the data and the GeoIntel Gi* grid can show it. Anomalies: 1 of 4 (Mangaluru cyber, z 2.36 in the final-8-week window); Mysuru chain snatching and Hubballi burglary produced negative-z alerts (−2.7, −2.36) in the window and Tumakuru vehicle theft none. Offenders: 38 of 200 planted repeat offenders have an identity-resolved PersonKey covering ≥ 50 % of their (name, case) rows, 2 fully; median coverage 0.33, median over-merge 6.75× — only cases with a co-accused are visible through NetworkEdge, so these are floors, and the card says so. Communities: 5 of 12 planted rings map ≥ 50 % of their members to one Louvain community (94 of 104 members resolved; 13 Louvain communities, largest 222).

**D-phase8-depth-7 · Spatial scopes are paged per month, not by one date-descending walk (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
The first trajectory read for Bengaluru City capped a 12-month scope at 3,000 rows ordered by date descending, which emptied the first four months and classed 19 cells "consecutive" — an artefact of the cap, not a pattern. `scopedPoints` now issues one paged query per month with an equal share of the budget and reports `perMonthCap` and `monthsTruncated`. Bengaluru City × Property Crimes (2,028 cases, complete): 33 hot cells — 7 persistent, 18 sporadic, 6 historical, 2 diminishing; stability 0.353; two-period (first six months against the last six): 18 persistent hot, 2 cooled, 3 intensified, 2 weakened, none new. The lattice and Gi* window are the client's `routes/geointel/stats.js` constants, so a cell scored here is the cell the grid layer draws; the panel defaults to Bengaluru City when the map has no district filter because a state-wide 2 km lattice makes every occupied cell "hot".

**D-phase8-depth-8 · Knox and festival uplift are reported as the data gives them (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
Bengaluru City × Property Crimes, last 6 months, 500 m × 14 days, 979 geocoded cases: 22.3 % originators, 38.6 % near-repeats, 3.3 % repeats, 35.9 % isolated, 167 chains (largest 31), Knox ratio 1.02 with permutation p = 0.28 — no space–time interaction beyond chance. That is the honest reading of a generator that plants stable places (spatial-hour hotspots) rather than contagion, and the panel says "as chance" rather than dressing it up. Festival uplift across all heads: +30.9 % (95 % CI 16.8–45.0, n = 9 festivals), pooled +35.0 %, against the generator's FESTIVAL_FACTOR of 1.35 — the estimator recovers the planted effect. Baselines exclude every other festival's ±3-day window because Dasara and Deepavali sit 18 days apart and one window would otherwise sit inside the other's baseline.

**D-phase8-depth-9 · Benchmark artefacts are bundled in the function, not read from docs/ (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
The deployed bundle is `functions/dappa_api` alone, so `docs/benchmarks/*.json` is unreachable at runtime. `pipeline/depth_benchmarks.py` writes byte-identical files to both `docs/benchmarks/` and `functions/dappa_api/lib/depth/bench/` and mirrors the pipeline's own `forecast_metrics.json` / `identity_metrics.json`; `lib/depth/bench.js` loads them with a null fallback so a build without the JSON still answers `/depth/benchmarks` with `meta.present` listing what exists.

**D-phase8-depth-10 · No feature flag for this phase (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
Every `/depth/*` answer is deterministic in-function statistics over existing tables; no Catalyst AI service is called, so there is nothing to gate and nothing to fall back from. `meta.source` is `'local'` and `meta.engine` `'in-function statistics'` on every response, and the suite asserts that `FEATURE_ZIA=on` changes nothing.

**D-phase8-depth-11 · Lead–lag is computed on first differences (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
Monthly counts share the generator's 4 % YoY drift and monsoon dip, so raw-level cross-correlation would call every head a "leader" of every other. Series are differenced month to month before correlating at lags 1–3, and a lead is claimed only when |r| ≥ 2/√n (0.338 at n = 35). Bengaluru City live: four leads clear the line (e.g. Cyber Crimes → Others at +2 months, r 0.55); the panel prints the noise line beside them.

**D-phase8-depth-12 · Rows left out, and why (phase 8, 28 Aug 2026).**
Row 201 (victim ⇄ accused overlap) needs Victim rows for the corpus cases — another 20–30 chunked reads per cold call on top of the corpus walk — and was cut for latency; row 204 (co-offending age structure per crew) and 205 (crew MO by year) need per-case ages and dated MO tags that the MO-derived crews (client-side, undated tags) do not carry; row 207 (cross-station gap chaining) and 191 (station-risk calibration) were not started — the risk table's 30-day horizon starts after the data window ends, so a true forward calibration is impossible on this dataset and a same-window proxy would need careful wording. The MO-vocabulary de-noising named in the brief already exists client-side (`routes/offenders/moVocab.js` learns a name lexicon from the profiles and reports the noise share); a server duplicate would not be a new feature.
