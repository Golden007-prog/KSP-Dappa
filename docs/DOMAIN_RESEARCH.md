# Domain research — what a real police analytics product does

*Round-2 research note for KSP DAPPA. Researched 27 August 2026. Scope: the Indian (and specifically Karnataka) crime-records ecosystem that DAPPA imitates — CCTNS/ICJS, NCRB "Crime in India", the Karnataka SCRB's own reports, station registers and beat practice, officer vocabulary, the statutory and constitutional limits on acting on a prediction, and the published record on predictive policing. Every claim carries the URL it came from and a verification tag.*

**Legend.** `[V]` VERIFIED — the sentence was read on the linked page, or extracted from the linked PDF, during this session. `[I]` INFERRED — taken from a search-engine snippet of a page that would not open, or a synthesis I draw from verified facts (the reasoning is shown). `[X]` the page was inaccessible (HTTP 403/404, DNS failure, certificate error) — nothing is claimed from it beyond its existence; the full list is in §10.

Two source caveats apply throughout. The **Karnataka Police Manual, 1998** is not online in full text; its orders are quoted second-hand from a Karnataka High Court judgment of 29 January 2026 and from Mrinal Satish's 2011 NLSIR article, both of which I read. The **Puducherry Police Manual** *is* online in full text and is used as a reference for the form of station registers, the daily occurrence report and the beat book; every such use is labelled "Puducherry manual" and is not a claim about Karnataka practice.

## 1. Key findings

1. **The state's own analytics product is the SCRB's Monthly Crime Review, and it is a *reported-count* document.** The July 2026 review (55 pages, "Crime Review and Performance of Units under CCTNS Project", issued by the Police Computer Wing & State Crime Records Bureau) reviews 17 heads month-on-month and year-on-year, tabulates 38 units, and only reaches detection/pendency in one table, the "Comparative Crime Statement 17". Its footer says "This Crime Review is based on classification made in Police IT at Police Station level" and "Statistics for the years 2025 & 2026 are provisional" `[V]` ([PDF](https://ksp.karnataka.gov.in/storage/pdf-files/CRIME%20REVIEW%20-%20JULY%20-%202026.pdf)). DAPPA's KPI vocabulary should mirror this document, not NCRB's.
2. **Karnataka's CCTNS application is "Police IT".** "All police stations in Karnataka run the PoliceIT software for their day to day operations. Key activities such as registration of FIR/ UDR/Missing, maintenance of case diaries, maintenance of key registers, passport verification and others are performed using the PoliceIT software" `[V]` ([Janaagraha/NCR Consultants, Police Station Process Document, Bengaluru, 2013](https://www.janaagraha.org/files/publications/Police-Station-Process-Document.pdf)). The Sakala report in the Crime Review says the department "has implemented 24 SAKALA Services through PoliceIT Application" `[V]`.
3. **NCRB counts by the Principal Offence Rule and disclaims authenticity.** "If many offences are registered in a single FIR case, only the most heinous crime i.e. the one that attracts maximum punishment will be considered as counting unit"; "NCRB is not responsible for authenticity of the information"; Crime Rate = cases per lakh population; Charge-sheeting Rate = (Cases Charge-sheeted / Cases disposed off by Police) × 100; Conviction Rate = (Cases Convicted / Cases in which Trials were Completed) × 100 `[V]` ([Crime in India 2023, Vol. I](https://www.ncrb.gov.in/uploads/files/1CrimeinIndia2023PartI.pdf)). A one-FIR-one-head counter is therefore the correct aggregation for any NCRB-comparable KPI.
4. **The statutory channel for "acting on a prediction" is not arrest but Executive-Magistrate security proceedings**, and the SCRB already reports them: the Crime Review's "Preventive Action Report" counts 126, 128 and 129 BNSS cases (July 2026: 2,550 / 240 / 1,808) `[V]`. Section 129 BNSS is "Security for good behaviour from habitual offenders" — "by habit a robber, house-breaker, thief, or forger", etc. `[V]` ([IndianKanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/106164203/)). BNSS s.170 permits preventive arrest only "if it appears to such officer that the commission of the offence cannot be otherwise prevented", capped at 24 hours `[V]` ([BNSS Ch. XII](https://bareacts.onlinelawconnect.com/actsandrules/criminal/bnss/chp12.php)).
5. **Karnataka's rowdy sheet is a supervised, reviewable list — and the High Court now requires a hearing.** KPM Standing Order 1059: "Prior Orders of the Superintendent of Police or the Sub-Divisional Police Officer should be obtained for entering the name of every rowdy"; Part A confirmed resident rowdies, Part B non-resident/homeless confirmed rowdies, Part C "Novices who are budding goondas"; a Part C name "may be struck off" after one year without entry; "No name should be struck off from Part 'A' or Part 'B' … without the order in writing of the Superintendent of Police" `[V]` ([Ravi v State of Karnataka, KHC, 29 Jan 2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/177181757/)). The court faulted the police for giving no "opportunity … to file his reply or explanation" `[V]`. Any DAPPA offender-level view must be built to feed that hearing, never to replace it.
6. **The Supreme Court's surveillance line is procedural, not prohibitive, but it is real.** *Gobind* (1975): domiciliary visits and picketing "should be reduced to the clearest cases of danger to community security and not routine follow-up at the end of a conviction or release from prison or at the whim of a police officer" `[V]` ([CIS](https://cis-india.org/internet-governance/blog/surveillance-and-the-indian-consitution-part-2)); *Puttaswamy* (2017) three-fold test — legality, legitimate state aim, proportionality `[V]` ([SC Observer](https://www.scobserver.in/reports/constitutionality-of-aadhaar-justice-k-s-puttaswamy-union-of-india-judgment-in-plain-english/)); *Pegasus* (2021): "indiscriminate spying on individuals cannot be allowed except with sufficient statutory safeguards" `[V]` ([IndianKanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/39021018/)).
7. **DPDP Act 2023 exempts law-enforcement processing but also exempts research/statistics.** s.17(1)(c): Chapters II–III and s.16 do not apply where "personal data is processed in the interest of prevention, detection, investigation or prosecution of any offence"; s.17(2)(b): the Act does not apply to processing "for research, archiving or statistical purposes" not used for individual-specific decisions `[V]` ([IndianKanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/180784190/)). DAPPA's synthetic, aggregate analytics sit squarely in 17(2)(b); anything person-specific would fall under 17(1)(c) and then under *Puttaswamy* proportionality `[I]`.
8. **Every major predictive-policing deployment that has been evaluated failed to show crime reduction.** Chicago SSL: "Individuals on the SSL … were neither more nor less likely to become a victim"; "did not have a significant city-level impact on homicide trends" `[V]` ([CEBCP summary of Saunders et al. 2016](https://cebcp.org/evidence-based-policing/the-matrix/individuals/individuals-saunders-et-al-2016/)). Dutch CAS: police stopped it "because it was impossible to prove that burglaries decreased due to the use of the system" `[V]` ([AlgorithmWatch](https://algorithmwatch.org/en/dutch-police-cas-predictive-policing/)); the official register lists it "Buiten gebruik" (out of use) from 1 December 2025 `[V]` ([Dutch Algorithm Register](https://algoritmes.overheid.nl/en/algoritme/81228922)).
9. **The feedback-loop critique is specific and reproducible.** Lum & Isaac: police data showed "200 times more drug-related arrests" in West Oakland/International Boulevard while "drug crimes are much more evenly distributed across the city"; simulated PredPol targeting would hit Black residents "at roughly twice the rate of whites" `[V]` ([Significance, 2016](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1740-9713.2016.00960.x)). The Karnataka analogue: the Crime Review's SLL heads are dominated by police-initiated cases (Karnataka Police Act gambling 9,309 YTD; Karnataka Excise Act 7,104 YTD; NDPS 7,508 YTD, Jan–Jul 2026) `[V]` — these must never feed a hotspot model `[I]`.
10. **India's flagship predictive system, Delhi CMAPS, was audited and found hollow.** CAG: CMAPS "largely fetches data from PA-100 only and complete integration with CCTNS was yet to be achieved (as of September 2019)"; "records made available to audit did not indicate any communication by Delhi Police with ADRIN after March 2018"; the project "witnessed diminished interest after initial stages" `[V]` ([CAG Report No. 15 of 2020, ch. 9](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2020/9.%20Digital%20Initiatives%20of%20Delhi%20Police-05f911198e45a25.81448827.pdf)). Marda & Narayan's ethnography found mapping "filters that can point out ghettos, migrant colonies and minority settlement areas" `[V]` ([FAT* 2020](https://www.shivanginarayan.com/fat2020-final586.pdf)).
11. **Delhi Police treats an 80% face-match as "positive".** "All matches above 80% similarity are treated as positive results while matches below 80% similarity are treated as false positive results"; "no legal opinion was sought prior to procuring facial recognition technology"; test identification parades "were not conducted for any of them" `[V]` ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/government/delhi-police-ai-facial-recognition)). This is the concrete Indian precedent for why DAPPA must have no auto-identification path.
12. **ICJS explicitly promises "Forecasting/Predictive Trends in Crimes reported region-wise, category-wise"** and "Pan-India criminal/accused name search in the regional language for improved inter-state tracking of criminal movement" `[V]` ([PIB factsheet, 23 June 2022](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2022/jun/doc202262367401.pdf)). DAPPA's forecast and identity-resolution features map onto stated national intent, which is a legitimacy argument judges will recognise.
13. **The station crime chart is the ancestor of DAPPA's hotspot map, and it carried calendar annotations.** The Puducherry manual's Part II "crime chart" must note "Full moon dates", "The dates of important festivals" and "The dates of shandies" because "outside criminals generally visit these areas during these festivals" `[V]` ([Puducherry Police Manual ch. XXXIV](https://police.py.gov.in/Police%20manual/Chapter%20PDF/CHAPTER%2034%20Police%20Station%20records.pdf)). DAPPA's festival-window annotations are therefore a genuine officer expectation, not a gimmick.
14. **Kannada officer vocabulary is verifiable from KSP's own Kannada pages and Kannada press** — e.g. ಮಾಸಿಕ ಅಪರಾಧ ಸಮೀಕ್ಷೆ (Monthly Crime Review), ಪೊಲೀಸ್ ಬೀಟ್ (police beat), ಯುಡಿಆರ್ ಪ್ರಕರಣ (UDR case), ರೌಡಿಶೀಟರ್ (rowdy sheeter), ಸರಗಳ್ಳತನ (chain snatching), ತಲೆಮರೆಸಿಕೊಂಡ ಆರೋಪಿ (absconding accused) `[V]` — see the glossary in §4 for each source.

## 2. The data plumbing: station → SCRB → NCRB, and ICJS

### 2.1 CCTNS

- NCRB "was entrusted with the responsibility for monitoring, coordinating and implementing the Crime and Criminal Tracking Network & Systems (CCTNS) project in the year 2009" `[V]` ([Digital Police portal, About](https://digitalpolice.gov.in/DigitalPolice/AboutUs)).
- Coverage as of 1 July 2021: deployed in "16,276 (100%)" police stations, "connectivity has been provided in 15735 (97%)"; "National Database has grown to about 28 Crores records"; "FIR forms (IIF-1) submitted in courts has shown an increase by 15% i.e. from 70 % to 85%" `[V]` (same page).
- Citizen services on the portal: "filing of complaints online and seeking antecedent verification", plus "search for missing persons, NOC for purchase of second hand vehicle and information on Proclaimed Offenders" `[V]` (same page).
- KSP's own page describes CCTNS as "a Mission Mode Project under the National e-Governance Plan (NeGP)" whose aim is "an IT-enabled-state-of-the-art tracking system around 'Investigation of crime and detection of criminals'" `[V]` ([KSP CCTNS page](https://ksp.karnataka.gov.in/new-page/cctns/en)).
- The NCRB-recommended **Integrated Investigation Forms** are, as listed verbatim in the Puducherry manual: "IF1 – First Information Report (under section 154 Cr.P.C.); IF2 – Crime details forms; IF3 – Arrest / Court surrender memo; IF4 – Property seizure memo; IF5 – Final Report From (Under section 173 Cr.P.C.); IF6 – Court disposal memo; IF7 – Result of appeal form" `[V]` ([Puducherry manual ch. XXXIV](https://police.py.gov.in/Police%20manual/Chapter%20PDF/CHAPTER%2034%20Police%20Station%20records.pdf)). The organiser's ER schema (CaseMaster → Accused → ChargesheetDetails → court disposal) is a direct descendant of IF1→IF3→IF5→IF6 `[I]`.
- Search snippets (page not opened) say NCRB's "Monthly Crime Statistics" software was released in December 2016 and the Crime in India / MCS proformae were revised in 2014 `[I]` ([NCRB fingerprint/CCTNS index page, per search snippet](http://ncrb.gov.in/indexfpcp.html)).

### 2.2 Karnataka's application: Police IT

- "All police stations in Karnataka run the PoliceIT software for their day to day operations … Inter station communication is also assisted by the PoliceIT software. Certain Police Constables are trained and deployed to assist the other staff in data entry, generation of reports and related activities. Such constables constitute the Computer wing." `[V]` ([Janaagraha process document](https://www.janaagraha.org/files/publications/Police-Station-Process-Document.pdf), p. 8).

- A search snippet attributes the Karnataka Police IT/CCTNS integration (electronic FIR and charge-sheet transfer to courts) to Wipro as developer; the underlying page was not opened `[I]` ([SlideShare "CCTNS Karnataka Overview", per snippet](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/dg-ncrbve13/33158448)).
- The public FIR search exposes four mandatory keys — District/Division (ಜಿಲ್ಲೆ ಆರಿಸಿ), Police Station, FIR Number, Year (ವರ್ಷ ಆರಿಸಿ), 2016–2026 — behind a CAPTCHA `[V]` ([KSP FIR search](https://ksp.karnataka.gov.in/firsearch/kn)). Bengaluru Rural's "Daily Crime Report" page is nothing more than a link to that search `[V]` ([Bengaluru District Police](https://bangaloreruralpolice.karnataka.gov.in/page/DAILY+CRIME+REPORT/en)).

### 2.4 What an SCRB submits to NCRB, and how "Crime in India" is built

All quotations from *Crime in India 2023*, Volume I front matter `[V]` ([NCRB PDF, 22 MB](https://www.ncrb.gov.in/uploads/files/1CrimeinIndia2023PartI.pdf)):

- Collection: "The annual State/UT data for 'Crime in India' is furnished by the 36 States/UTs in the NCRB prescribed proformae through a software application developed by NCRB"; "The data is entered by State/UT Police at Police Station/District level"; "The consolidation of District/State level data is done by State Police Agencies (SCRB/CID)"; "First level data validation is done at the Police Station/District level … Second level data validation is done at the state level at the time of consolidation"; "Data is scrutinized at NCRB for discrepancy / inconsistency".
- Metropolitan cities: data "for 53 Metropolitan Cities by respective SCRBs/CIDs", 19 of them (population > 20 lakh) published in hard copy.
- Disclaimer: "NCRB is not responsible for authenticity of the information."
- Principal Offence Rule (quoted in §1).
- Terms: "'Cases Registered', 'Cases Reported', 'Number of Cases', 'Number of Crimes', 'Number of incidences', 'Incidences' are interchangeably used to indicate the number of registered FIRs."
- Limitation 2: "The Socio-economic causative factors or reasons of crimes are not being captured by the Bureau." (DAPPA's socio-economic correlation panel is therefore something NCRB does *not* do; it must be labelled indicative `[I]`.)
- Crime rates use 2022 mid-year projected population (2011-census based) for states, and actual Census 2011 for cities; "Hence, Crime Rates of States and Metropolitan cities are not comparable."
- Headline 2023 figures: 62,41,569 cognizable crimes (37,63,102 IPC + 24,78,467 SLL); crime rate 422.2 (2022) → 448.3 (2023); IPC charge-sheeting rate 72.7%.

Volume structure (from the Contents pages) `[V]`: Vol. I — Ch. 1 Summary (All-India, States/UTs, Metropolitan Cities, Violent Crimes), Ch. 2 Murder and Kidnapping & Abduction (motives, victims, recovery), Ch. 3 Crime Against Women, Ch. 4 Crime Against Children, Ch. 5 Juveniles in Conflict with Law, then "Glossary / Definitions / Abbreviations"; Vol. II — Ch. 6 Senior Citizens, Ch. 7 SCs/STs, Ch. 8 Economic Offences & Corruption, Ch. 9 Cyber Crimes, Ch. 10 Offences against the State / Extremists, Ch. 11 Environment; Vol. III — Ch. 12 Railways, Ch. 13 Foreigners, Ch. 14 Human Trafficking, Ch. 15 Missing Persons, Ch. 16 Custodial Crimes & Complaints against Police / Police casualties & firing, Ch. 17 Disposal of Cases by Police, Ch. 18 Disposal of Cases by Courts, Ch. 19 Arrests, Convictions & Acquittals and Recidivism, Ch. 20 Property Stolen & Recovered and Seizures. Each thematic chapter has "Police Disposal … (Crime Head-wise)" and "Court Disposal …" tables (e.g. 3A.5–3A.8 for women) `[V]`. The open-data mirror lists the same tables as CSVs, e.g. "Motives of Murder - 2023", "City-wise Property Stolen and Recovered during 2023", "Arrests under IPC Crimes (Crime Head-wise) - 2023" `[V]` ([data.opencity.in](https://data.opencity.in/dataset/crime-in-india-2023)).

Critique of the compilation (ORF) `[V]` ([Measuring Crime in India](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/measuring-crime-in-india-the-limits-of-statistical-compilation)): four under-reporting mechanisms — NCRB "covers only cognisable offences"; the Principal Offence Rule; "crime burking" (police avoid registering FIRs "to show lower jurisdiction crime rates"); and citizen non-reporting "due to stigma, fear, or police harassment". DAPPA's anomaly detector should treat a sudden *drop* in registrations at one station as a data-quality flag as much as a rise `[I]`.

### 3.3 Station-level registers (Bengaluru, 2013 process study)

From the NCR Consultants / Janaagraha "Police Station Process Document, Part A – Process Mapping, March 2013" `[V]` ([PDF](https://www.janaagraha.org/files/publications/Police-Station-Process-Document.pdf)):

- Functional organisation of a station: S.H.O.; Station Sentry; Reception; Writer Section; Investigation Section split into "Law & Order", "Crimes", "UDR, Missing & Petitions"; Beat Staff; Women Cell; Passport Section; Janasnehi Police; Process Staff; Computer Wing; Court Duty Staff; Cheetah; Hoysala.
- Intake: "All the information pertaining to the complaint is recorded first in the station house diary. At this stage, the complaints are identified as cognizable or non-cognizable complaints." Non-cognizable → "Non-cognizable Register"; "All petty cases are entered in the Petty Cases Register and other miscellaneous crimes are entered in the Crime Miscellaneous Register"; "Under Sakala, acknowledgements have to be issued within one working day of receipt of the complaint."
- Documents named: PSD-01 Station House Diary ("Manual and Computerized"), PSD-02 Non-cognizable Register, PSD-03 Petty cases Register, PSD-04 Criminal Miscellaneous Register, PSD-05 C Misc./NCR Acknowledgement, PSD-08 Seized Property list, PSD-11 Case Diary ("A Police officer investigating any crime has to keep a record of what all he did in the course of investigation"), PSD-12 Property Seized Register, PSD-14 UDR Register ("A register of information of all the unnatural death cases registered"), PSD-17 Missing Register; also "Prisoners Search Register", "Progress diary and Court Disposal diary" kept by court-duty staff.
- Patrol assets: "Hoysala (Police patrol car) and Cheetah (Police patrol bike) are given to beat staff for patrolling work. Janasnehi Police points are identified in the jurisdiction of every police station."
- Bengaluru's City Crime Record Bureau "collects data of crimes and criminals and maintains a database", handles "service and execution of summons and warrants", and "Publication matters relating to Missing persons, Lost and Found Vehicles, Unclaimed property" `[V]` ([BCP CCRB](https://bcp.karnataka.gov.in/9/city-crime-record-bureau/en)).

The Puducherry Part I annual crime review objectives are worth quoting because they are, verbatim, DAPPA's product brief: "(i) to give a brief but comprehensive account of the salient features of the crime of the year, (ii) to analyse the increasing trend of crime in a particular station of the circle, (iii) to estimate the sources of undetected crime, (iv) to indicate to the SHO the ways and means of dealing with the crimes, and (v) to give to SHO and his staff a scheme for preventive activities"; "The possibility of the cases occurring in different areas of his circle forming a series and being contacted should be carefully scrutinized"; the review "should be of practical value and not a mere catalogue of the crime in the year" `[V]`.

### 3.4 The beat system and the beat book

- Karnataka's "New Beat System" took effect "with effect from 1st April, 2017"; features: "Empowering police men; Decentralisation of policing; Strengthening the community policing; Connecting the community with the police; Making policing more effective; Making policing services more approachable" `[V]` ([KSP Police Beat](https://ksp.karnataka.gov.in/new-page/police%20beat/en)); Kannada page headed ಪೊಲೀಸ್ ಬೀಟ್, "ಸಮುದಾಯ ಪೊಲೀಸಿಂಗ್ ಅನ್ನು ಬಲಪಡಿಸುವ ನಿಟ್ಟಿನಲ್ಲಿ" `[V]` ([KSP, Kannada](https://ksp.karnataka.gov.in/new-page/police%20beat/kn)).
- "each and every Head Constable / Police Constable in the Police Station has been assigned with a specific jurisdiction as his/her area of responsibility"; "Each Beat is having a number of citizens as the 'Beat Citizen Committee Members' This number is on an average around 30 in each Beat", in contact "24X7 … either over voice call or SMS OR through Whatsapp message by maintaining a Whatsapp group" `[V]` ([Mysuru City Police](https://mysurucitypolice.karnataka.gov.in/info-2/Police+New+Beat+System/en); [Vijayapura District Police](https://vijayapurapolice.karnataka.gov.in/info-1/Police+New+Beat+System/en)).
- A search snippet (page not opened; the old Mysore City Police host no longer resolves) lists beat-officer powers as serving summons and warrants, police verification, enquiry on complaints, investigating cases, reviewing rowdy elements and gathering intelligence `[I]` ([mysorecitypolice.gov.in, per snippet](http://mysorecitypolice.gov.in/contentpage.aspx?id=80)).
- Bengaluru: "a network of beats manned by foot patrol and vehicle patrol during day and night", "special mobile patrol units called the Hoysala, traffic patrol and a number of armed striking force mobile units"; "Each zone is headed by an Officer of the rank of Deputy Commissioner of Police. Each zone is sub-divided into sub-divisions headed by Assistant Commissioners of Police which are further divided into Police Stations under the charge of an Officer of the rank of Police Inspector" `[V]` ([BCP overview](https://bcp.karnataka.gov.in/6/overview/en)).
- Beat book, reference model (Puducherry manual order 1493, verbatim) `[V]`: constables go on beat "(a) to gather information in villages, (b) to watch specially dangerous criminals or criminal organisations which require surveillance, (c) to pick up information from or about travelers or strangers …, (d) to make enquiries in particular villages whether bad characters have been in the neighbourhood, and (e) to ascertain any definite place of information and serve processes"; "an SHO himself should march and receive the beats and check the beats at least thrice a week"; "A cross check of the entries in the GD, KD check register, history sheets, exconvict check register and the pocket note books should be made to see the correctness of entries in the beat book" ([ch. XXXIV](https://police.py.gov.in/Police%20manual/Chapter%20PDF/CHAPTER%2034%20Police%20Station%20records.pdf)). Karnataka's beat book format itself was not found online `[X]`.

## 6. Statutory and constitutional constraints on acting on a prediction

### 6.1 BNSS 2023, Chapter XII "Preventive action of the police" (ss. 168–172)

Verbatim/summary from [BNSS ch. XII](https://bareacts.onlinelawconnect.com/actsandrules/criminal/bnss/chp12.php) `[V]` and [IndianKanoon s.168](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/193066845/) `[V]`:

- s.168: "Every police officer may interpose for the purpose of preventing, and shall, to the best of his ability, prevent, the commission of any cognizable offence."
- s.169: "Every police officer receiving information of a design to commit any cognizable offence shall communicate such information to the police officer to whom he is subordinate …"
- s.170(1): arrest without warrant of "the person so designing, if it appears to such officer that the commission of the offence cannot be otherwise prevented"; s.170(2): no detention "exceeding twenty-four hours".
- s.171: interpose to prevent injury to public property; s.172: persons "bound to conform to the lawful directions of a police officer".

Reading for DAPPA `[I]`: the chapter authorises *presence and prevention* on information; it does not authorise arrest on a statistical forecast, because s.170 requires knowledge of a specific "design" and a finding that nothing short of arrest will prevent it. A hotspot is grounds for patrol allocation (s.168), not for detention.

### 6.2 Security proceedings before the Executive Magistrate (BNSS ss. 125–143) — the lawful "habitual offender" channel

- s.126 "Security for keeping peace in other cases" — "When an Executive Magistrate receives information that …" `[V]` ([IndianKanoon s.126](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/153745901/)); s.128 "Security for good behaviour from suspected persons" `[V]` ([IndianKanoon s.128](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/125761744/)); s.129 "Security for good behaviour from habitual offenders": persons who "(a) is by habit a robber, house-breaker, thief, or forger, (b) is by habit a receiver of stolen property …, (c) habitually protects or harbours thieves …, (d) habitually commits … kidnapping, abduction, extortion, cheating or mischief …, (e) habitually commits … offences involving a breach of the peace, (f) [habitual offences under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, Foreigners Act, Customs Act, Food Safety and Standards Act, or hoarding/profiteering/adulteration laws], or (g) is so desperate and dangerous as to render his being at large without security hazardous to the community" — bond "not exceeding three years" `[V]` ([IndianKanoon s.129](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/106164203/)).
- The Karnataka Crime Review already counts these as "Preventive Action Report" (July 2026: 126 BNSS 2,550; 128 BNSS 240; 129 BNSS 1,808; total 4,598, vs 5,223 in June 2026 and 3,124 in July 2025) `[V]`.
- The Puducherry DOR likewise reports "Sections 107, 109, 110 Cr.P.C." (the CrPC predecessors) as a daily line `[V]`.

Reading for DAPPA `[I]`: if a repeat-offender view is to lead anywhere lawful, it leads to an SP/SDPO-approved rowdy-sheet entry and, at most, a s.128/129 proceeding *decided by an Executive Magistrate* with a show-cause hearing — the system's output can only be a dossier for that hearing.

### 6.3 Arrest safeguards

- BNSS s.35(1)(b): for offences punishable up to seven years, arrest only if the officer has reason to believe the offence was committed *and* is satisfied arrest is necessary (to prevent further offence, for proper investigation, to prevent tampering, to prevent inducement of witnesses, or to ensure court attendance), and "The police officer shall record while making such arrest, his reasons in writing"; s.35(3) notice of appearance where arrest is not required; s.35(5) a person who "complies and continues to comply with the notice … shall not be arrested"; s.35(7) "No arrest shall be made without prior permission of an officer not below the rank of Deputy Superintendent of Police" for infirm or over-sixty persons in sub-three-year offences `[V]` ([IndianKanoon s.35](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/75059398/)).
- *Arnesh Kumar v State of Bihar* (2014) directions `[V]` ([IndianKanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/2982624/)): "All police officers be provided with a check list containing specified sub-clauses under Section 41(1)(b)(ii)"; "The police officer shall forward the check list duly filed and furnish the reasons and materials which necessitated the arrest"; "The Magistrate … only after recording its satisfaction … will authorise detention"; "Failure to comply … shall apart from rendering the police officers concerned liable for departmental action, they shall also be liable to be punished for contempt of court."

### 6.4 Absconders

- BNSS s.84 (quoted in §4): court-issued proclamation, ≥30 days, publicly read and affixed, optional newspaper publication; "proclaimed offender" declaration only for grave offences `[V]` ([IndianKanoon s.84](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/148501062/)).
- Manual practice (Puducherry, verbatim): absconding accused "entered in red ink" on the final-report form with a request for a non-bailable warrant; then ss. 82/83 CrPC proceedings; "As long as the proclaimed offender's name is on the list of proclaimed offenders of his station, he and his station staff are responsible for finding out where he is and who is harbouring him" `[V]` ([ch. XXXVIII(H)](https://police.py.gov.in/Police%20manual/Chapter%20PDF/CHAPTER%2038%20H%20Completion%20of%20Investigation%20and%20Final%20Report.pdf)).

### 6.5 Karnataka-specific instruments

- **Karnataka Police Act 1963, s.55 "Removal of persons about to commit offences"** `[V]` ([Act text](https://policehumanrightsresources.org/content/uploads/2016/02/Karnataka-Police-Act-1963.pdf?x80005=)): in Bengaluru and other commissionerate areas the Commissioner, elsewhere the District/Sub-Divisional Magistrate, may act where "(a) … the movements or acts of any person are causing or calculated to cause alarm, danger or harm to person or property, or (b) … there are reasonable grounds for believing that such person is engaged or is about to be engaged in the commission of an offence involving force or violence or an offence punishable under Chapter XII, XVI or XVII of the Indian Penal Code … and when in the opinion of such officer witnesses are not willing to come forward to give evidence in public against such person by reason of apprehension", by "an order in writing duly served on him" directing conduct or removal from the area. s.56 covers persons *convicted* of listed offences. s.163 "Power of Government to make rules" `[V]`; Satish notes "The Karnataka Police Manual gets its legal basis from § 163 of the Karnataka Police Act, 1963" `[V]` ([NLSIR](https://docs.manupatra.in/newsline/articles/Upload/B06B8036-FE1B-42C0-AF68-0BF08990BF3E.pdf)).
- **Karnataka Prevention of Dangerous Activities of Bootleggers, Drug-offenders, Gamblers, Goondas, Immoral Traffic Offenders and Slum-Grabbers Act, 1985** (the "Goonda Act"): s.3(1) "The State Government may, if satisfied with respect to any bootlegger or drug-offender or gambler or goonda … that with a view to prevent him from acting in any manner prejudicial to the maintenance of public order, it is necessary so to do, make an order directing that such persons be detained"; maximum "twelve months from the date of detention" (s.13) `[V]` ([LegitQuest](https://www.legitquest.com/act/karnataka-prevention-of-dangerous-activities-of-bootleggers-drug-offenders-gamblers-goondas-immoral-traffic-offenders-and-slum-grabbers-act-1985/6E78)). The Crime Review carries a zero-count row for it under "Karnataka State Local Acts" `[V]`.
- **Karnataka Police Manual 1998, Standing Order 1059 (Register of Rowdies) and 1060** `[V]` ([Ravi v State of Karnataka, 29 Jan 2026](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/177181757/); [Satish 2011](https://docs.manupatra.in/newsline/articles/Upload/B06B8036-FE1B-42C0-AF68-0BF08990BF3E.pdf)): definition and Parts A/B/C (§1 finding 5); "All the Criminal activities of the rowdy including reasonable suspicion of his complicity in cases … should be mentioned in separate paragraphs which should be numbered chronologically"; SHO to check daily that SHD rowdy entries are carried into the Register (Rule 1059(8)); photographs of rowdies shown at roll call (1059(17)); Order 1060 (search snippet): history sheets retained to end-December, then a superior officer's orders for continuance `[I]`. Other rules quoted by Satish: 1036 Village Crime Register of "all individuals against whom there is a suspicion"; 1045 records of unconvicted persons only while "reasonable suspicion" exists; 1049 convicted names removed ten years after last sentence; 1052(1) history sheets for persons "known or … believe[d] to be addicted to … crime"; 1058 suspect history sheets "only at the discretion of the Superintendent of Police"; 1062(1) first offender from "a family of criminals" designated "professional criminal"; 1063(4) first-time BC is "close-watch" `[V]`. The 2026 High Court judgment quashed a rowdy-sheet entry because no "opportunity was given to the petitioner to file his reply or explanation", citing *K. M. Muniswamy Reddy* — "if any of the Fundamental Right is to be affected by the State action, it has to be authorised by law" `[V]`. A 2025 CaseMine commentary on *Sunil Kumar @ Silent Sunil v State of Karnataka* on the same due-process point exists but returned 403 `[X]` ([CaseMine](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/in/due-process-and-police-surveillance-of-%E2%80%9Crowdy-sheeters%E2%80%9D:-commentary-on-sunil-kumar-@-silent-sunil-v.-state-of-karnataka/view)).
- Satish's conclusion on digitising these lists: "Maintenance of archaic 'history sheets' and 'rowdy sheets' is an anathema in a constitutional democracy like India" and CCTNS "aims to computerise these records to facilitate sharing of data" `[V]`.

### 6.7 Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023

- s.17(1): Chapter II (except s.8(1) and 8(5)), Chapter III and s.16 do not apply where "(c) personal data is processed in the interest of prevention, detection, investigation or prosecution of any offence or contravention of any law"; s.17(2): the Act does not apply to "(a) State instrumentalities notified by Central Government" for sovereignty/security and "(b) processing for research, archiving or statistical purposes" not used for individual-specific decisions `[V]` ([IndianKanoon](https://indiankanoon.org/doc/180784190/)).
- Commentary (search snippet): unlike UK/French law, s.17(1)(c) "does not incorporate any limitations based on the nature of the investigation or investigating authority" `[I]` ([Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas blog](https://disputeresolution.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2025/01/internal-investigations-under-the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023/)).
- Reading for DAPPA `[I]`: the statutory exemption removes consent/notice duties, not the constitutional proportionality test; and aggregate, synthetic, non-individual analytics are outside the Act entirely under 17(2)(b). The product's legal comfort therefore comes from staying aggregate.

## 7. The documented record on predictive policing

### 7.1 Place-based: PredPol and the feedback loop

Lum & Isaac, "To predict and serve?" (*Significance*, 2016) `[V]` ([Wiley](https://rss.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1740-9713.2016.00960.x)): Oakland drug arrests "concentrated in neighbourhoods around West Oakland (1) and International Boulevard (2)" with "200 times more drug-related arrests than areas outside of these clusters", while survey-based estimates "suggest that drug crimes are much more evenly distributed across the city"; "the presence of bias in the initial training data can be further compounded as police departments use biased predictions to make tactical policing decisions"; additional observed crimes "feed into the predictive policing algorithm on subsequent days, generating increasingly biased predictions"; "black people would be targeted by predictive policing at roughly twice the rate of whites". HRDAG's summary: machine learning on such data will "recycle the bias embedded in existing police practice while obscuring the problems with that practice" `[V]` ([HRDAG](https://hrdag.org/2016/10/10/predictive-policing-reinforces-police-bias/)).

### 7.2 Person-based: Chicago's Strategic Subjects List

- RAND quasi-experiment (Saunders, Hunt & Hollywood 2016): "426 highest-risk individuals for gun violence were selected and placed on a Strategic Subjects List"; SSL subjects "were neither more nor less likely to become a victim of a shooting or homicide than individuals in the comparison group"; "individuals in the treatment group were more likely to be arrested for a shooting" (attributed to greater surveillance); "the program did not have a significant city-level impact on homicide trends"; "This study does not indicate that the predictive policing approach used here helped to reduce gun violence in Chicago" `[V]` ([CEBCP](https://cebcp.org/evidence-based-policing/the-matrix/individuals/individuals-saunders-et-al-2016/); RAND page 403 `[X]` [RAND](https://www.rand.org/pubs/external_publications/EP67204.html)).
- Upturn's analysis of the released list: "398,684 individuals"; "287,404 have scores over 250"; "127,524 individuals on the list have never been arrested or shot, and around 89,160 of that group have a score greater than 250"; "age accounts for roughly 89% of variance in SSL scores"; "the top 1,400 people … were involved in less than 20 percent of the city's total gun violence in 2016" `[V]` ([Upturn](https://www.upturn.org/work/how-strategic-is-chicagos-strategic-subjects-list/)).

### 7.3 Netherlands: Crime Anticipation System (2015–2025)

- Official register: 125×125 m grid; "gecombineerd logistisch regressiemodel"; "CAS maakt gebruik van de informatie uit politieregistraties. Alle informatie is gedepersonaliseerd en anoniem gemaakt"; outputs processed by the team's information hub "waarbij zij ook gebruik maken van andere politie-informatie" to advise deployment; noted risk that absence of citizen reports "doesn't necessarily indicate no police presence is needed"; legal basis Politiewet arts. 3, 8, 13 and Wpg; status "Buiten gebruik", discontinued 1 December 2025 `[V]` ([Algoritmeregister](https://algoritmes.overheid.nl/en/algoritme/81228922)).
- AlgorithmWatch: Police Academy evaluation found CAS "correctly predicted about 13,5 % of the areas in which burglaries took place" but "no significantly lower crime rate" in CAS units; a 2025 internal report: "Only one out of 50 incidents in the city of Amsterdam was correctly predicted"; the "indicator on the number of non-Western immigrants" was removed in 2017; stopped "because it was impossible to prove that burglaries decreased due to the use of the system" `[V]` ([AlgorithmWatch](https://algorithmwatch.org/en/dutch-police-cas-predictive-policing/)); NL Times report 403 `[X]` ([NL Times](https://nltimes.nl/2026/02/27/dutch-police-discontinue-controversial-crime-predicting-algorithm-cas)).

### 7.4 India

- **Delhi CMAPS.** CAG (Report No. 15 of 2020, ch. 9): MoU with ISRO-ADRIN signed December 2015; "primarily envisioned as a Decision Support System"; four phases ("Crime analytics module, Security module (target threat rating, situation database creation), News Module (Geo tagging, clustering) and Social media and siterips (Social network analysis, Text annotation)"); "CMAPS largely fetches data from PA-100 only and complete integration with CCTNS was yet to be achieved (as of September 2019)"; advanced features "have not been implemented"; no communication with ADRIN after March 2018; training constables "lacks justification since CMAPS is to be used by senior management"; the audit recommends "a third-party evaluation … to ascertain the efficacy/impact assessment of surveillance-based policing" `[V]` ([CAG](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2020/9.%20Digital%20Initiatives%20of%20Delhi%20Police-05f911198e45a25.81448827.pdf)). Marda & Narayan (FAT* 2020): "little known about the effect of CMAPS due to the lack of public accountability mechanisms and large exceptions for law enforcement under India's Right to Information Act"; input from "the Dial 100 call centre"; existing mapping has "filters that can point out ghettos, migrant colonies and minority settlement areas"; PCR van officers "negotiate with their Dispatch counterparts into being assigned fewer investigations"; HQ officers argue "most of the people who call from slums and ghettos would not do so if the calls to the number 100 were chargeable" `[V]` ([paper PDF](https://www.shivanginarayan.com/fat2020-final586.pdf); ACM page 403 `[X]` [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3351095.3372865)). Vidhi: "very little is known about the effect of the CMAPS due to large exceptions for law enforcement agencies under the Right to Information (RTI) Act" `[V]` ([Vidhi](https://vidhilegalpolicy.in/blog/indias-tryst-with-predictive-policing/)). SFLC: "FIRs in India are frequently under-reported for certain categories of crime … the data used to 'train' predictive systems often mirrors existing social and institutional biases"; "This creates a feedback loop where increased surveillance and policing are justified by algorithmic outputs that are themselves shaped by prior bias"; RTI requests "frequently denied … on grounds of commercial confidence or blanket exemptions" `[V]` ([SFLC](https://sflc.in/tracing-the-rise-of-predictive-policing-in-india/)). LSE blog: Muslims, Dalits and Adivasis "constitute over 50% of undertrial prison population as compared to their 39% share in general population" `[V]` ([LSE](https://blogs.lse.ac.uk/humanrights/2021/04/16/predictive-policing-in-india-deterring-crime-or-discriminating-minorities/)).
- **Delhi facial recognition.** "over 750 cases related to the North East Delhi riots"; 80% threshold (§1 finding 11); "no legal opinion was sought"; TIPs "not conducted for any of them"; deployment "will almost inevitably disproportionately affect Muslims, particularly those living in over-policed areas like Old Delhi or Nizamuddin" `[V]` ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/government/delhi-police-ai-facial-recognition)); RTI reply reported as treating >80% as positive and <80% as "false positive", used for the 2020 riots, the 2021 Red Fort violence and Jahangirpuri `[V]` ([Outlook India](https://www.outlookindia.com/national/delhi-police-s-80-per-cent-match-of-facial-recognition-to-nab-accused-raises-concern-finds-rti-news-217182)); IFF's RTI page 403 `[X]` ([IFF](https://internetfreedom.in/project-panoptic-right-to-information-updates/)).
- **Punjab PAIS.** "database of over 90,000 criminals"; "facial recognition, gang analysis and phonetic search"; FICCI Smart Policing Award 2018; yet "hardly used by cops performing duties at checkpoints" `[V]` ([The Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/punjab/smart-policing-nailing-criminals-not-a-handicap-anymore-309154)); the 84,000-sample voice bank claim is from a Statesman page that returned 403 `[X]` ([The Statesman](https://www.thestatesman.com/india/punjab-police-deploys-ai-powered-pais-to-dismantle-gangster-networks-creates-84000-sample-voice-bank-1503588695.html)). Marda & Narayan note PAIS "received a Smart Policing Award for its use of facial recognition" `[V]`.
- **Hyderabad / Telangana.** Command and Control Centre "supports the processing of data from up to 600,000 cameras at once"; "There is currently no safeguarding legislation which would protect the privacy of the citizens of Hyderabad, nor a law which would regulate the use of remote biometric surveillance"; May 2021 videos of police asking civilians "to remove their masks, to obtain a biometric capture" `[V]` ([Amnesty, Ban the Scan](https://banthescan.amnesty.org/hyderabad/)); TSCOP (2018) "assists investigating officers in detecting faces of crime suspects and missing persons by cross-referencing them with the CCTNS database" `[V]` ([SFLC](https://sflc.in/tracing-the-rise-of-predictive-policing-in-india/)); Telangana's "360 degree view" of citizens `[V]` ([FAT* 2020](https://www.shivanginarayan.com/fat2020-final586.pdf)).
- **NCRB AFRS.** Tender of 8 July 2019 "to establish the system" for "criminal identification, verification and its dissemination among various police organizations"; NCRB's defence that it "automates the existing police procedure of comparing suspects' photos" `[V]` ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_Facial_Recognition_System_(India))).
- **The pre-digital critique.** Satish 2011 on Karnataka's suspect lists: "just on the basis of suspicion, which is sometimes based on the person's socio-economic status and caste, a person is presumed guilty by the police and no due process rights are provided"; in *Malak Singh* the petitioner "was considered a suspect in every case being investigated by the police in that area" after listing `[V]` ([NLSIR](https://docs.manupatra.in/newsline/articles/Upload/B06B8036-FE1B-42C0-AF68-0BF08990BF3E.pdf)).

## 8. Predictive-policing safeguards checklist for DAPPA

Each item names the fact or authority it answers to. "Implement" items are things the Round-2 build can actually ship; none of them inflates the feature count unless it delivers visible substance.

1. **Purpose binding — aggregate deployment support only.** Hotspots, forecasts and anomaly alerts are produced for patrol allocation and supervision (BNSS s.168 lane), never as grounds for individual suspicion. Basis: BNSS s.170's "cannot be otherwise prevented" standard; DPDP s.17(2)(b) statistical exemption; *Puttaswamy* proportionality. *Implement:* a one-line purpose statement on every analytic screen and in the PDF brief; an API contract that no endpoint returns a per-person risk score.
2. **No auto-identification, no biometrics, no FRT.** The Offender-360 view links *already-registered accused* in FIR records (record linkage), and says so; it does not nominate "budding goondas". Basis: KPM 1059 Part C critique; Chicago SSL (a third never arrested); Delhi's 80% face-match rule and missing TIPs. *Implement:* rename any "risk" label on persons to "record-linkage confidence"; strip photo/face fields from any pipeline; assert this in `/meta/services` copy.
3. **Human-in-the-loop with a named rank.** Any person-level artefact must be reviewable by the rank the manual already assigns — SP/SDPO for rowdy-sheet entries, an Executive Magistrate for s.126–129 — and every alert must be acknowledged by an identified user. Basis: KPM 1059 "Prior Orders of the Superintendent of Police or the Sub-Divisional Police Officer"; KHC 2026 hearing requirement; MeitY "human oversight remains central". *Implement:* the existing `POST /alerts/:id/ack` should record rank + unit + free-text reason; unacknowledged alerts expire.
4. **Statutory routing, not action prompts.** Where the UI mentions a person, it links the lawful next step (BNSS s.35 checklist and *Arnesh Kumar* directions for arrest; s.84 for absconders; s.126–129 proceedings; KPA s.55 externment; Goonda Act) and never says "arrest" or "detain". *Implement:* a static "lawful next steps" panel with the section texts and the Arnesh Kumar checklist.
5. **Audit trail with retention and destruction rules.** Log who viewed which person/hotspot and why; purge person-level views on a schedule that mirrors the manual's own clocks (Part C one year; history sheets reviewed each December; PUCL "destroyed when it was no longer needed"). *Implement:* a Catalyst Data Store `AuditLog` table written by the API middleware; a retention job in `dappa_nightly`.
6. **Feedback-loop guard in the training set.** Hotspot and forecast models use complainant-reported heads (property, body, women/children) and exclude police-initiated heads — Karnataka Police Act gambling, Excise, NDPS, COTPA, 126–129 BNSS, MV Act — which measure enforcement effort, not victimisation. Basis: Lum & Isaac; CAS's "absence of reports" caveat; ORF on burking. *Implement:* an explicit allow-list of crime heads in `pipeline/analytics.py` and a visible "trained on" list in the Predict screen.
7. **Bias monitoring as a first-class output.** Report the share of hotspot-hours and alerts landing on each district's socio-economic class, and flag when a station's *registration* drops abnormally (possible burking). Caste and religion stay out of every feature (already policy). *Implement:* one disparity panel; one "registration collapse" anomaly type.
8. **Transparency stamps in the SCRB's own idiom.** Every figure carries "as on <date>", "provisional", the data window, and the method (backtest MAPE, CI); NL→ZCQL reveal stays. Basis: Crime Review footers; NCRB disclaimer; Marda & Narayan on RTI opacity. *Implement:* a `meta.provenance` block on every API response, rendered in the UI.
9. **Data minimisation and synthetic-only.** No real PII, no scraped social media or "siterips" (the CMAPS modules CAG found abandoned). *Implement:* the existing synthetic banner plus a "sources" page listing exactly which ER tables feed each screen.
10. **Pre-registered success criteria and a sunset.** State, before any pilot, what would count as success (e.g. burglary reduction in hotspot beats vs matched controls) and when the model is retired if it fails. Basis: CAS was stopped because it "was impossible to prove" benefit and had "no clear goals"; RAND found no city-level impact. *Implement:* an "Evaluation plan" section in the Weekly Brief and in `docs/DECISIONS.md`.
11. **Contestability artefact.** For any person shown in Offender-360, one click exports the underlying FIR/chargesheet references so the person's counsel or the SDPO can check them at the rowdy-sheet hearing the High Court now requires. *Implement:* a "record summary" print view.
12. **Vocabulary alignment.** Use the Crime Review's heads and the CCS-17 disposal states (Reported/True/False/Under Investigation/Undetected/Pending Trial/Convicted/Acquitted/Abated) in KPIs, so an SP reads DAPPA the way they read the SCRB. *Implement:* a KPI dictionary page and consistent labels in `client/`.

## 9. What this means for the Round-2 build

- **A "Monthly Crime Review" generator is the most credible single addition.** Replicate the SCRB's 17-head structure, the current-vs-previous-vs-same-month-last-year triad, the fixed prose sentence, the unit-wise breakup for rare heads, the 38-unit table and the range/commissionerate rollup — from DAPPA's synthetic data — and ship it as the SmartBrowz PDF. Judges from KSP will recognise the form instantly `[I]`.
- **Add the two SCRB panels DAPPA lacks:** a Preventive Action Report (126/128/129 counts by unit) and a CCS-17-style disposal funnel (Rep → True → UI/UN/PT → Con/Acq/Abated) `[I]`.
- **Keep, and label, the festival/full-moon annotations** on the trend and hotspot views: they are a manual-era expectation (Puducherry crime chart), not decoration (`[V]` for the source, `[I]` for the recommendation).
- **Give each tier its own landing view** (beat tile, station console, district command, state command) with the daily questions in §5; the CAG's point that CMAPS training targeted the wrong rank is a warning about who the product is for `[I]`.
- **Ship the safeguards visibly**: purpose statements, "trained on" lists, provenance stamps, the lawful-next-steps panel and the audit log are cheap, and they are what distinguishes a decision-support tool from the systems in §7 `[I]`.
